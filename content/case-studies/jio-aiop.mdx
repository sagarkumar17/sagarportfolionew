# Jio AI Operations Platform

**Jio Platforms · Operations Platform**

*A no-code platform for configuring and operating welfare schemes.*

A guided workspace helps Program Officers configure a scheme, validate its rules, publish it and monitor how it performs.

> Journey: Research baseline · design complete

## The brief

### Scheme setup is an operating lifecycle

**The problem.** Program teams had to configure scheme rules, map required data and documents, validate eligibility, publish campaigns and monitor applications across fragmented workflows. The supplied evaluation flags weak feedback, reversibility and guidance.

**The solution.** A guided scheme lifecycle uses templates, editable steps, data-to-document mapping, eligibility simulation, campaign preview and post-publish oversight.

**The outcome.** The design connected scheme setup, validation, publishing and ongoing oversight into one guided workflow. The completed design established a clear foundation for usability validation and implementation.

A welfare scheme is an operational system, not a single application form. Program teams need to define the scheme, decide who is eligible, connect each data point to evidence, publish a public-facing service and monitor what happens after launch. The existing work was spread across manual checks and disconnected steps. AIOP was intended to let teams configure schemes and targeted services without technical expertise while keeping eligibility, verification and oversight connected.

## Research & discovery

### Six usability gaps shaped the scheme lifecycle

| Heuristic review | 6.5/10 baseline | Six observed usability themes |
| Workflow review | Scheme lifecycle | Configure → validate → publish → monitor |

I began with the operating model and the current product rather than with a screen redesign. The work combined a heuristic evaluation of EasyGov AIOP, public-scheme benchmarking across EasyGov, mPariksha, SevaSetu, MyScheme, DBT Bharat and UMANG, and workflow research around the Program Officer’s responsibilities. I also benchmarked creation patterns from Google Ads, Facebook, Microsoft, LinkedIn and Amazon, looking at guided setup, templates, audience controls, previews and performance visibility. User stories and workflow synthesis covered targeting, eligibility validation, campaign creation, approvals, alerts and analytics. The heuristic evaluation scored the current experience 6.5/10 and identified six connected usability gaps.

## What the research revealed

### Five findings shaped the direction

#### 01 / Guidance is a control

Program Officers configure eligibility and public-facing rules. Progress, editable steps and contextual help reduce the chance of an incomplete setup.

#### 02 / Validation belongs before publication

Teams need to test eligibility rules, data-to-document mappings and campaign output before applicants encounter errors.

#### 03 / A scheme needs review and accountability

Creation, verification and approval are different responsibilities. The workflow needs review states, actionable feedback and a route to revise.

#### 04 / Publishing is the midpoint

Once a scheme is live, teams need visibility into applications, drop-off, performance and disbursal signals so they can intervene.

#### 05 / Context must persist

Users should not have to remember selected data, required documents or earlier decisions while moving through a long configuration flow.

## Design strategy

### The design response

- Guide complex setup in a clear sequence.
- Keep scheme, data and document context visible across steps.
- Validate rules and previews before anything goes live.
- Make review feedback actionable and preserve a way to revise.
- Design post-publish dashboards around intervention, not passive reporting.

The design reframed the product from “create and publish” to “configure, validate, govern and improve.” Templates provide a starting point; a stepper makes the sequence legible; grouped data points connect to required documents; an eligibility simulator tests rules; and the post-publish workspace supports ongoing management. A proposed creator → verifier → Program Manager → disbursement workflow adds checkpoints, while keeping each handoff reviewable and editable.

## Research synthesis

### One guided path from scheme setup to oversight

A guided lifecycle gives scheme teams a way to configure, check and manage a program without losing context between steps.

## Key decisions

### Make rules visible before a scheme goes live

#### Heuristic review

- **What we learned:** Back/edit paths and error feedback were weak.
- **Design response:** Make stages editable and surface errors earlier.

#### Workflow review

- **What we learned:** Rules need to be checked before publication.
- **Design response:** Add an eligibility simulator and pre-publish validation.

#### Mapping review

- **What we learned:** Data and supporting documents were hard to relate.
- **Design response:** Group data points and show their document mapping.

#### Governance model

- **What we learned:** Creation and approval responsibilities differ.
- **Design response:** Validate proposed creator → verifier → manager → disbursement roles.

## What we delivered

### From research through release

The visual design covered a lifecycle from dashboard and scheme templates through basic details, data and document selection, eligibility simulation, campaign preview, approval and post-publish scheme management. The scheme workspace included the ability to inspect applicants, activity, KPIs and settings, as well as report, clone or unpublish. The project reached visual design; usability testing with Program Officers and developer handoff were the next stages. The approval model is presented as a proposed workflow for validation, not as a tested operational policy.

## Outcomes & evidence

### The redesign started from a 6.5/10 usability baseline

A heuristic review identified six usability themes. The design phase is complete; user validation follows.

The verified outcome at this stage is the completed end-to-end design and a clearer framework for evaluating the platform. There are no post-launch business results in the project material, so impact is represented by the 6.5/10 heuristic baseline, the six gaps addressed in the design, and a next-phase measurement plan: scheme-creation task success, time to configure, rule errors caught before publication, approval turnaround and application drop-off.

## Reflection

### What I learned and what I would do next

Working on AIOP reinforced that a strong enterprise flow must carry its logic across setup, approval and operations. I would validate the highest-risk decisions first—the data/document map, eligibility simulator and role handoffs—and instrument the lifecycle before implementation so the team has a comparable baseline.
