# PayU Partners Platform

**PayU · Partner Program**

*Moving partner onboarding from KAM dependency toward self-service.*

A self-service partner experience makes it easier to refer merchants, follow onboarding and understand incentives without relying on a KAM for routine updates.

> Journey: Partner onboarding · reported outcome

## Meta

- **Role:** Lead Product Designer
- **Platform:** Web · partner dashboard
- **Timeline:** Oct 2021–Jun 2022
- **Scope:** Partner research · task-based IA · onboarding · status and earnings
- **Type:** B2B

## The brief

### Partner growth depended on self-service

**The problem.** Partners needed a KAM to onboard merchants, understand KYC status or verify incentives. “TBD” timelines and an IA organized around PayU’s internal structure made self-service difficult.

**The solution.** Research reframed the dashboard around partner jobs: add merchants, track status, understand earnings, find resources and manage the account.

**The outcome.** The project reported 40% lower onboarding drop-off and onboarding completion three times faster after the self-service experience was introduced.

PayU’s partner program connected hundreds of active partners to a large merchant network, but its dashboard did not let partners complete basic work independently. Adding a merchant, checking KYC progress and understanding incentives often required a Key Account Manager. That dependency consumed support time and made onboarding feel unpredictable. The design brief said “redesign the platform”; the research reframed the problem as a structural gap in self-service, information architecture and trust.

## Research & discovery

### Partners needed progress, timing and earnings to be visible

| Method | Focus | Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 groups | Interview coverage | Active, new, stuck, enterprise partners, internal sales |
| 7 stages | Partner journey | Signup through incentive payout |
| 4 themes | Pain-point synthesis | Awareness · conversion · usage · retention |
| 3 structures | IA exploration | Card sorting / prototypes tested with partners |
| Support evidence | Tickets + KAM logs | Identify recurring assistance requests |

I interviewed 14 people across five groups: active resellers, new resellers, partners stuck partway through onboarding, enterprise platform partners and internal sales stakeholders. Including partners who had stopped was deliberate; successful users alone could not explain where the experience broke down. I paired the interviews with support-ticket and KAM-call analysis, mapped needs against each journey stage, and traced the lifecycle from signup to incentive payout. The work documented more than 60 pain points and grouped them into awareness, conversion, usage and retention themes. Claude supported transcript clustering; the resulting themes were checked against the interview and support evidence.

## What the research revealed

### Five findings shaped the direction

#### 01 / Stuck partners showed the structural failure

Abandonment was not a cosmetic issue. People could not complete tasks or understand what would happen next without help.

#### 02 / “TBD” damaged confidence

KYC status without a credible time range left partners unable to set expectations for their merchants. Uncertainty created avoidable follow-up.

#### 03 / Navigation reflected PayU, not partner work

The original 11-item structure followed internal product groupings. Partners thought in tasks: add a merchant, track it, understand earnings and get help.

#### 04 / KAM calls were a symptom

Routine questions about status and progress pointed to missing self-service information, rather than a preference for phone support.

#### 05 / Earnings needed an explanation

A total without visible calculation made incentives hard to verify and generated another reason to contact support.

## What people said

> “Where is my merchant?”

> — A recurring partner question documented in the KAM-call analysis. It pointed to missing self-serve onboarding status.

## Design strategy

### The design response

- Organize around partner tasks rather than internal product taxonomy.
- Replace vague statuses with credible next steps and time ranges.
- Explain earnings at the merchant and transaction level.
- Let partners add and follow merchants without a KAM handoff.
- Keep support visible for exceptions while reducing routine dependency.

The strategy was to make the dashboard self-serve by default and make escalation the exception. The information architecture moved from 11 product-based items to five partner jobs: Add Merchants, Track Status, Earnings, Resources and Account. The journey became a connected pipeline, with clear timelines and blockers, transparent incentive calculations and KAM support available when a case genuinely needed intervention.

## What we delivered

### From research through release

The design work moved through partner interviews and journey mapping, pain-point synthesis, card sorting and prototype testing of three information architectures, then low- and mid-fidelity onboarding explorations. The final platform direction brought together merchant addition, KYC progress, pipeline blockers, earnings breakdowns, resources and account management. KAMs shifted from the default path for status updates toward an escalation role. The project materials report the new task-based IA as a pattern adopted by other PayU B2B products.

## Outcomes & evidence

### Self-service made onboarding faster and more complete

The project reports lower onboarding drop-off and faster completion after the platform redesign.

The project reports onboarding drop-off falling by 40% and completion becoming three times faster. It also describes lower routine KAM contact and reuse of the task-based IA elsewhere in PayU. The metrics frame the outcome around the original problem: more partners could move a merchant through onboarding without needing a person to explain the platform at every step.

## Reflection

### What I learned and what I would do next

Recruiting partners who had abandoned the process made the research more actionable than a study of successful users alone. I would instrument the onboarding funnel before redesign, align KAMs earlier on the shift to exception support, and phase the new IA with migration help for existing partners.
