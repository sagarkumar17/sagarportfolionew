# PayU Merchant Super App

**PayU · Merchant Super App**

*One merchant workspace for payments, products and business operations.*

A merchant-first app brings payment activity, product discovery and everyday business tools into one place—so merchants can act without switching between app and web dashboard.

> Journey: 125,560 app users · product discovery and engagement · trailing 365 days

## Meta

- **Role:** Product Design · UX Research · Usability Testing
- **Platform:** Android & iOS · PayU merchant app
- **Timeline:** Dec 2022–Jun 2023
- **Scope:** Merchant research · product analytics · app IA · discovery · usability testing
- **Type:** B2B

## The brief

### Merchants needed one useful operating workspace

**The problem.** Merchants lacked a clear mobile workspace for payments and business tasks. Product discovery and engagement were weak: in the prior 365-day cohort, 83,040 of 125,560 app users visited Reports or Products, 62,300 tried a product, and 40,850 engaged with one. The existing experience also under-served invoicing, payment handles, support and cross-platform continuity.

**The solution.** Reframe the app around merchant jobs: an actionable home, quick access to common tasks, clearer reports and product entry points, and a connected path into PayU products. Use cross-sell and quick actions to make relevant capabilities discoverable without crowding the core payment experience.

**The outcome.** The design work delivered a merchant-focused app direction grounded in a 125,560-user analytics cohort, interviews across app-only, web-only and cross-platform merchants, and usability testing. The supplied Figma case study does not provide verified post-launch outcome metrics, so the 32% figure is presented as the research baseline rather than a redesign result.

PayU’s existing merchant app sat alongside a web dashboard and a growing set of products. Merchants used these channels differently, while business discovery showed falling app engagement and low awareness of available tools. The project brief was to make the mobile experience more useful for day-to-day operations and give merchants a clearer path from payment activity into relevant business products.

## Research & discovery

### Research connected merchant behavior to the product gap

| Method | Focus | Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary research | In-person + phone interviews | Existing merchants who used app only, web dashboard only, or both |
| Analytics cohort | 125,560 app users | Past 365 days; 83,040 visited Reports or Products |
| Product funnel | 66% → 50% → 32% | Visited products/reports → tried a product → engaged with a product |
| Usage mix | 74 / 18 / 8 | Payment Links / Invoices / Payment Handle |
| Business signal | 20K MAU decrease | October to December 2022; product engagement also down 12% |

I started with business discovery and the existing product analytics, then spoke with merchants who used the mobile app only, the web dashboard only, or both. That sampling was important: the redesign had to support different channel habits rather than assume every merchant worked from a phone. The analytics covered 125,560 app users over the previous 365 days. 83,040 (66%) visited Reports or Products, 62,300 (50%) tried at least one product, and 40,850 (32%) engaged with a product. Payment Links represented 74% of product usage, Invoices 18%, and Payment Handle 8%. The Figma case study also records a 20,000 decrease in MAU between October and December 2022 and a 12% decline in product engagement. These signals shaped the interview questions and prioritization; they are baseline evidence, not measured redesign impact.

## What the research revealed

### Five findings shaped the direction

#### 01 / Discovery was not the same as adoption

Two thirds of the cohort visited Reports or Products, but only 32% engaged with a product. A visible entry point alone did not guarantee repeat value.

#### 02 / One product dominated use

Payment Links represented 74% of product usage, while Invoices accounted for 18% and Payment Handle 8%. Adjacent capabilities needed clearer, contextual discovery.

#### 03 / Merchants moved across channels

Interview recruitment deliberately included app-only, web-only and combined users. Mobile needed to complement the dashboard rather than duplicate it blindly.

#### 04 / The home screen had an activation role

The Figma research connects product-section churn to missing quick actions and recommends quick actions and cross-sell as discovery opportunities.

#### 05 / Engagement was already weakening

The source records a 20K MAU decline from October to December 2022 and 12% lower product engagement, making repeat utility a business concern.

## Design strategy

### The design response

- Protect the core payment task while making next actions discoverable.
- Place quick actions close to the merchant’s current context.
- Cross-sell a product when its value relates to the task at hand.
- Treat mobile and web as connected parts of one merchant service.
- Measure both product discovery and sustained engagement after launch.

The strategy was to make the home a useful operating surface, not just an account summary. Lead with payment health and actionable tasks, make frequent actions one tap away, and introduce adjacent products where they solve a relevant merchant need. Keep reporting and product discovery coherent, and ensure that merchants who rely on the web dashboard can continue their work across channels.

## Key decisions

### Make the next useful merchant action easy to find

#### Product analytics

- **What we learned:** Only 32% of app users engaged with a product after discovery and trial.
- **Design response:** Reduce the distance between the home screen and useful merchant actions.

#### Usage mix

- **What we learned:** Payment Links accounted for 74% of product use; invoice and payment-handle use was smaller.
- **Design response:** Keep the primary task clear while surfacing adjacent tools contextually.

#### Merchant interviews

- **What we learned:** Merchants used app, web, or both; capabilities were uneven across channels.
- **Design response:** Design mobile and web as connected parts of the same merchant workflow.

#### Business review

- **What we learned:** 20K MAU decrease from October to December 2022; product engagement fell 12%.
- **Design response:** Prioritize activation, product awareness and repeat utility before adding breadth.

## Outcomes & evidence

### The largest opportunity was product discovery

PayU app analytics over the previous 365 days. The funnel describes the existing experience before the redesign; it is not a post-launch result.

The strongest demonstrated outcome is the evidence-backed redesign direction: a merchant workspace organized around core payment work, product discovery and cross-channel use. The 32% product-engagement figure is the pre-redesign baseline for the 125,560-user cohort. A post-launch comparison should track the same funnel, MAU and product mix to show whether quick actions and contextual cross-sell changed behavior.

## Reflection

### What I learned and what I would do next

This project made the distinction between reach and useful engagement concrete: visiting the product area did not mean a merchant adopted or returned to a tool. I would instrument discovery, first successful task and 30-day repeat use by channel and merchant segment before release, then compare the same measures after launch.
