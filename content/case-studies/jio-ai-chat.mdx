# Jio AI Chat

**Jio Platforms · Conversational AI**

*Turning a failing support chatbot into an intent-aware assistant.*

A support assistant designed to understand common customer needs, give clearer answers and make human help easier to reach.

> Journey: Recharge · plans · network · bills · SIM activation

## The brief

### Support had to work at Jio scale

**The problem.** A linear decision tree did not recognize intent, held no session memory, and hid agent escalation 4–5 taps deep. Text-heavy flows and missing feedback made it hard to know what was failing.

**The solution.** A hybrid assistant pairs quick replies for common queries with open text for edge cases, warmer responses, a persistent agent path and per-message feedback.

**The outcome.** Three months after launch, the project reported CSAT rising from 2.6 to 4.2, call volume falling by 55–60% and average resolution time dropping below 90 seconds.

Jio serves hundreds of millions of customers across India, making digital support an important first point of contact. The existing chatbot had become a costly dead end: people who could not complete a task often moved to phone support, while the service team had little structured feedback about where the conversation failed. The goal was to improve resolution, trust and access to human help without making the assistant slower for common requests.

## Outcomes & evidence

### A focused intent model improved support outcomes

Outcomes reported three months after launch across the Jio app ecosystem.

Three months after launch, the case-study reports CSAT increasing from 2.6 to 4.2, call volume reducing by 55–60%, and average resolution taking under 90 seconds. The team also reported fewer repeat contacts and incorporated response feedback into quarterly model retraining. These results connect the interface to the support operation: shorter paths helped people resolve common tasks, and the feedback loop gave the team a way to improve answers over time.

## Research & discovery

### Five high-volume needs shaped the conversation model

| Method | Focus | Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 weeks | Discovery period | Heuristic review, interviews, tickets and analytics |
| 12 | User interviews | Jio users across age groups and geographies |
| 3 × n=4 | Concept tests | Card-tree, AI-first and hybrid paradigms |
| Top 5 = 68% | Query concentration | Recharge, plans, network, bill payment, SIM activation |
| 3× | Quick-reply performance | Reported in moderated testing; confirm task and measure |
| 4–5 → 1 tap | Agent access | Source describes moving escalation closer |

I spent three weeks understanding both sides of the support experience before changing the interface. I reviewed the existing conversation against Nielsen’s usability heuristics, interviewed 12 Jio users across age groups and geographies, analyzed support tickets, compared Jio with Airtel Thanks, Vi and global AI support patterns, and reviewed analytics for session length, drop-off and resolution by flow. I then tested three low-fidelity conversation models with four people each: a visual card tree, an AI-first chat and a hybrid. Claude helped cluster interview transcripts; I reviewed the themes against the source material. Figma AI helped lay out competitor comparisons and generate early screen variations for discussion.

## What the research revealed

### Five findings shaped the direction

#### 01 / The need was concentrated

Five intents—recharge, plans, network, bills and SIM activation—accounted for 68% of queries. A small set of clear entry points could serve a large share of support demand.

#### 02 / The escape route was hidden

“Talk to an agent” was one of the most sought-after actions, yet took four to five taps to reach. People who could not find help abandoned the conversation.

#### 03 / Correct answers still felt robotic

Users over 40 could receive an accurate response and still leave because the tone did not make them feel understood. Trust depended on how the answer was delivered.

#### 04 / Text-heavy flows excluded people

Users aged 45+ had higher drop-off in text-led paths. Card-based quick replies performed three times better in moderated testing.

#### 05 / There was no learning loop

Without message-level feedback, the team could not see which answers failed or why. A feedback control needed to connect experience quality with model improvement.

## Design strategy

### The design response

- Resolve the task, not maximize chat time.
- Keep the agent path visible in every conversation state.
- Use warm, plain language and explain what the assistant is doing.
- Offer visual shortcuts for frequent requests and accessible touch targets.
- Attach feedback to each response so weak answers can be found.
- Design the conversation model and interface together; test language and AI limits early.

The strategic move was from a decision-tree navigator to an intent-aware assistant: keep common needs predictable and fast, let people describe less common problems in their own words, and make human help visible at every point. The hybrid concept won in testing because it supported both quick selections and open-ended questions without forcing every user into one interaction style.

## Research synthesis

### Five intents, one hybrid assistant

A hybrid model preserved predictable shortcuts for frequent needs while leaving room for open-ended requests.

## What we delivered

### From research through release

The work covered the entry state, active conversation, response loading, unresolved questions, agent handoff, session resume, resolution summary and dark mode. I also defined reusable chat bubbles, quick-reply chips, typing and loading states, and response feedback components. The design shipped across MyJio, JioTV and JioCinema, with the components reused by three other Jio product teams. The team included UX, technology and front-end leads, so the conversation states and the data needed for feedback were considered alongside the visual design.

## Reflection

### What I learned and what I would do next

The work showed me that a conversational interface is only as useful as its recovery paths. I would involve the model team from the first concept sprint, bring voice and regional-language testing into v1, and establish the analytics dashboard before launch rather than six weeks afterward.
