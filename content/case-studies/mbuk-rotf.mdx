# Retail of the Future Platform

**Mercedes-Benz UK · Retail of the Future**

*Equipping Mercedes-Benz UK retailers for an agency-model future.*

A retailer platform connects vehicle ordering, product information, campaigns and support for Mercedes-Benz UK’s move to an agency model.

> Journey: Manual process → validated digital order flow

## Meta

- **Role:** Product Designer
- **Platform:** Retailer and HQ platform · B2B
- **Timeline:** Design Jan–Apr 2021 · phased rollout through 2023
- **Scope:** Retailer research · ordering · publications · role-based platform
- **Type:** B2B

## The brief

### The agency model changed retailer work

**The problem.** Vehicle ordering took about two hours manually and had a 23% error/rejection rate. Retailers also lacked timely access to publications, inventory and support while Mercedes-Benz shifted toward a direct agency model.

**The solution.** A role-aware retailer platform combined ordering, publications, inventory, search, help and support, with validation and access governance across up to 12 backend systems.

**The outcome.** Following rollout, order placement fell from about 120 minutes to under 25. The platform reported 90% task success, satisfaction rising from 70% to 85%, and 10,000 vehicles sold through ROTF.

Mercedes-Benz UK was moving from a traditional franchise model toward an agency model. Mercedes-Benz would become seller of record, own inventory and set prices; retailer partners would focus on customer experience rather than carrying local stock risk. The existing work was fragmented across manual ordering, publications, inventory and support. Retailers needed accurate information while serving customers, and Head Office needed to govern confidential content and keep the network aligned.

## Outcomes & evidence

### Digital ordering reduced time and error opportunities

The reported rollout connected faster ordering with task success, satisfaction and platform use.

The project reports order time falling from about 120 minutes to under 25, meeting the original 80% reduction goal. It also reports 90% task success and 85% satisfaction during rollout (up from 70%), 10,000 vehicles sold through ROTF, and 125,000 users at full release in December 2023, including 96,000 monthly active users. A 23% error/rejection rate was the pre-design baseline; it is not represented as a measured post-launch reduction in the source.

## Research & discovery

### The agency model changed what retailers and HQ needed

| Method | Focus | Detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Analytics | Existing platform use | Traditional flow dominated; invoicing/payment features had near-zero adoption |
| 12+ interviews | Retailer + HQ ecosystem | Retailers, admins, staff, support and Head Office |
| Competitor scan | Toyota · Nissan · Fuso | Dealer portal capability matrix |
| Business context | Agency-model change | Map how inventory, pricing and selling responsibilities shift |
| Prioritization | MoSCoW | Orders, publications, access as “Musts”; analytics/forum later |

I began with analytics and business context, then spoke with 12+ people across retailers, administrators, staff, support and Head Office. Analytics showed that the traditional sales flow dominated while newer invoicing and payment features had near-zero adoption. I benchmarked Toyota Central Media Hub, Nissan Dealer Portal and Fuso Dealer Web Portal, and mapped the needs of two core users: a retailer who needs current campaigns, stock and orders at the counter, and a Head Office manager who publishes information and governs access. Statements about two-hour ordering, errors, inventory and delayed communication were translated into opportunities and measurable goals.

## What the research revealed

### Five findings shaped the direction

#### 01 / Manual ordering was slow and error-prone

Retailers spent about 120 minutes placing an order, with a 23% error or rejection baseline. Prepopulation and validation became a direct design priority.

#### 02 / Real-time inventory mattered in the agency model

Retailers could no longer rely on local stock buffers. Order flows needed to reflect Mercedes-Benz UK’s central inventory.

#### 03 / Publications were operational tools

Late or hard-to-find campaigns and product information left retailers unprepared during customer conversations.

#### 04 / Access had to match business roles

Confidential prices and documents required role/entity permissions, MFA or SSO, download limits and access records.

#### 05 / A platform had to reduce task switching

Ordering, content, support and requests needed to sit in one place across up to 12 backend systems.

## What people said

> “It takes around 2 hours to place a single order manually.”

> — Retailer statement recorded in the supplied case-study source. It led to validation and error checking at the point of entry.

## Design strategy

### The design response

- Keep customer-facing retailer work one step away.
- Make publications searchable, current and tailored to preferences.
- Validate orders against inventory as information is entered.
- Make confidential access rules visible and auditable.
- Give retailers a self-serve route to help and query tracking.

The design reframed the platform as a retailer workspace for selling, not a set of process forms. Task-based navigation and entity tabs kept cars, vans and parts accessible; publications became searchable and preference-aware; digital ordering used pre-populated information and inventory validation; and support requests could be raised and tracked within the platform. MoSCoW prioritization kept real-time ordering, publications and role-based access in the first release while deferring later opportunities such as forums and advanced analytics.

## Key decisions

### Bring ordering, information and support into one place

#### Analytics

- **What we learned:** Retailers relied on traditional sales; new features were barely used.
- **Design response:** Bring core tasks into one job-led home.

#### Interview synthesis

- **What we learned:** Late information and weak stock visibility hurt the sales conversation.
- **Design response:** Make publications searchable, timely and preference-aware.

#### Process baseline

- **What we learned:** Manual ordering took ~120 minutes with 23% errors/rejections.
- **Design response:** Prepopulate data and validate against live inventory.

#### Governance needs

- **What we learned:** HQ manages confidential rates and network-wide content.
- **Design response:** Use role/entity permissions, MFA/SSO and audit trails.

## What we delivered

### From research through release

The work spanned the retailer home, entity switching, quick actions, publication hub and details, live search and filters, digital ordering, campaign publishing, access controls and help/support. Usability studies with retailers and Head Office admins informed a phased rollout: 30% of UK retailers first, followed by full release. The platform integrated multiple vendor systems and provided the shared workspace needed for the new operating model.

## Reflection

### What I learned and what I would do next

Mapping the business-model shift before designing individual features helped connect ordering, content and access decisions. I would build compliance checkpoints into the design cadence as requirements changed, investigate low-adoption invoicing earlier and establish post-launch measurement before the long gap between 2021 design and 2023 rollout.
